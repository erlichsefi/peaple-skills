# Bring Cake, Say No Cake (אל תביאו כלום)

## Overview

Two rules that look like they contradict each other, but are actually one system:

- **Host:** "אל תביאו כלום" ("don't bring anything")
- **Guest:** always brings something

The host's "don't bring anything" is not an instruction. It's a ritual opening line.
The guest who actually brings nothing has failed the test, and will be talked about.

## Host mode

### When to use

You've invited anyone, for anything: a Friday dinner, a birthday, "just coffee".

### Workflow

1. **Invite**, and immediately add: "ואל תביאו כלום, באמת, יש הכל" ("and don't bring anything, really, we have everything").
2. **Repeat it at least twice** before the event. Once more when they arrive.
3. **Prepare food for 3x the number of guests.** "Just coffee" means coffee, two cakes, burekas, a vegetable platter and fruit.
4. **When they arrive with something, act surprised.** "למה הבאתם? אמרתי לכם!" ("why did you bring something? I told you!")
5. **Put what they brought on the table** right next to your own, slightly better version of the same thing.
6. **At the end, force them to take leftovers.** They will not leave with less food than they came with.

### Portion calculation

```
food_needed = guests × 3 + "מה אם יבואו עוד" (what if more people show up)
```

If there are no leftovers at the end, there wasn't enough food, and that's a disgrace.

## Guest mode

### When to use

You've been invited. The host said "don't bring anything". You've heard this, and you're bringing something.

### Workflow

1. **Ignore "don't bring anything".** It was said for the record.
2. **Choose what to bring** (see the Approved items table).
3. **When handing it over, say:** "זה סתם משהו קטן" ("it's just something small"), even if it's a three-layer cake.
4. **When the host protests, answer:** "מה, אני אבוא בידיים ריקות?" ("what, I'm going to come empty-handed?")
5. **Take the leftovers.** Refusing leftovers is the guest-side version of bringing nothing.
6. **Return the container**, full. An empty container is never returned empty.

### Approved items

| Occasion | Bring |
|---|---|
| Friday dinner | Wine, or a cake from the bakery the host likes |
| Just coffee | Rugelach. Always rugelach. |
| Kid's birthday | A gift and a cake, even if they said "no gifts" |
| New apartment | Bread and salt, plus a plant that will die in 3 weeks |
| Shiva | Food for 30 people |

## The container cycle

Containers are never returned empty. This means a container you received full must be returned full,
which obligates the other side to return it full again, forever.

Some containers in Israeli families have been in circulation since the 80s. Nobody remembers whose they originally were.

## Common mistakes

- **Host believing the guest when they say "we already ate".** Serve anyway.
- **Guest taking "don't bring anything" literally.** Will be mentioned at the next family event.
- **Bringing the same thing the host made.** Now there are two of the same cake, and a silent competition at the table.

## Red flags

- The host said "don't bring anything" and also sent a list of what to bring. This is a potluck in disguise. Follow the list.
